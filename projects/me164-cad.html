ME 164 — NX CAD & Mechanical Design

CategoryCAD / Coursework
SoftwareSiemens NX
TimeframeJan – May 2026

Siemens NX 3D CAD Modeling Mechanical Assemblies GD&T Engineering Drawings Fit & Tolerance Analysis McMaster-Carr Integration Manufacturing Documentation Design for Manufacturing

Overview

Through ME 164, I developed experience designing mechanical components and assemblies using Siemens NX, with an emphasis on engineering drawings, geometric dimensioning and tolerancing (GD&T), and manufacturability. I applied ANSI/ASME and ISO standards to communicate part dimensions, fits, and functional requirements through industry-style manufacturing documentation. Throughout the course, I designed and documented several mechanical components, including a custom wheel rim and a gerotor-based elbow air engine.

FEATURED MODELS
Project A

Wheel Rim

Objective: Recreate and modify a physical wheel rim as a fully parametric 3D CAD model and produce an engineering drawing for manufacturing.

Design Process

I began with a basic cylindrical sketch and used measurements from an existing physical wheel rim as the basis for the model. I built the geometry in Siemens NX using sketches, extrusions, revolves, and other parametric features to reproduce the rim's overall shape and thickness.

Engineering Considerations

After completing the 3D model, I created an engineering drawing containing the necessary dimensions and tolerances to communicate the design for manufacturing. This process gave me experience translating a physical object into a constrained CAD model while considering how dimensions and tolerances affect the final manufactured part.

Project B

Gerotor Elbow Air Engine

Objective: Modify an existing elbow air engine design by developing a custom rotary mechanism and complete mechanical assembly.

Gerotor Design

For the final project, my partner and I were tasked with modifying an existing flywheel-style elbow air engine into our own design. We chose to replace the original mechanism with a gerotor, a compact positive-displacement rotary mechanism that uses an inner and outer rotor to convert fluid flow into rotary motion. I designed the mechanical components and assembly in Siemens NX, paying particular attention to the interfaces between moving and stationary components.

Component Selection

Because the engine was intended to function as a physical assembly, we also had to consider component clearances, fits, fasteners, and the compatibility of commercially available components. To make the design physically realizable, I selected and integrated commercially available components from McMaster-Carr, including O-rings, ball bearings, machine keys, and wedge-lock washers. I incorporated the manufacturer's dimensions into the CAD assembly to ensure that the custom-designed components properly interfaced with the purchased hardware.

Tolerance & Assembly

The project required applying both CAD modeling and mechanical design principles, rather than simply creating visually accurate geometry. Component fits, tolerances, clearances, and assembly constraints all had to work together for the mechanism to function as intended.
